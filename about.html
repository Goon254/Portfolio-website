## The Actual Origin Story // Snake Xenzia to Next.js

Grew up in Eldama Ravine, Kenya. Childhood was soccer with cousins, herding livestock, helping my parents on the farm, and playing Snake Xenzia on my dad's Nokia. I was 6 when I touched a phone for the first time. I was hooked.

First real computer experience: my dad brought his work laptop home. We watched our first movie through VLC player. I played video games. That moment I knewcomputers and software were my thing.

People assume I learned to code in Kenya with slow internet. Nope. By 2021, Kenya had 5G—faster than parts of the US. I moved to Manchester, NH in December 2021 for university, and that's where I wrote my first line of code: cout << "Hello World" << endl; in January 2022. // Intro to Programming, SNHU

## Running & Breaking // 26:30 8K to hip injuries

Started running seriously in 2018 in high school, then joined Solian Training Camp where I realized I was actually good at this. Made it to NCAA Division II Cross-Country at SNHU, running 50-60 miles per week despite injuries.

Best race: 26:30 for 8K. Worst race: 33:30 for 10K at regionals with a hip and knee injury. Running teaches you endurance applies to everything—debugging included. Now I'm retired from competitive running (injuries), focused on lifting, swimming, and shipping code.

## Why I Built What I Built // The real project origin stories

ThletIQ: My friend Allan asked me to make him a workout plan. Instead of writing one, I built an entire platform. The opportunity cost was simple—pay $20/month for a subscription or gain experience building my own tool. I needed structure for the gym anyway. Win-win.

East African Bites: I love cooking chapati. People always compliment me on it. Most people work and don't have time to cook. I saw the opportunity—cook for them, deliver to them. People pay for convenience.

Diaspora Link: I'm an immigrant. Finding housing, jobs, rides, and people from home was really hard when I got here. WhatsApp groups are too cluttered. Diaspora Link unifies everything so immigrants can settle into society without the chaos. This was a personal need that turned into something others needed too.

Heaven Targeter Ministries: Met the client at church. They saw my East African Bites website and asked if I could build something for their organization. Learned a lot about client work: be organized, pay attention to details, be patient, be consistent, and deliver what you promise.

I never pull all-nighters. Sleep is too important. When code breaks at 2 AM, I play PUBG mobile, sleep, then come back to it with a fresh mind. I use AI a lot to debug—it's been incredibly useful.
